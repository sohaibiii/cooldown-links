The short version

On Ice has no account, no analytics, no advertising and no third-party code. There is no bank connection, no purchase history, no price tracking and no affiliate link anywhere in it. Everything it knows is written to your iPhone or iPad and stays there.

The apps you pick in Apple's Screen Time picker reach On Ice as opaque tokens that only your device can interpret. There is no app name, no identifier, and nothing to send.

There is exactly one exception, and it is off until you switch it on: the optional AI reflection. With Apple Intelligence as its writer it runs on your device and nothing leaves. With OpenAI as its writer, a short, fixed list of details leaves — listed in full below. Your journal never leaves the device, whichever writer you choose.

This policy applies to the On Ice app for iPhone and iPad, published by TOPSOL, and to this website.

What stays on your device

On Ice keeps the following in its own private storage on your iPhone or iPad, protected by your device's passcode and encryption. None of it is uploaded, synced to iCloud, backed up by us, or visible to us.

  • Your shops — apps and categories as opaque Screen Time tokens On Ice cannot read, and websites you added by address as that address (see below).
  • Your list — the item names you typed, the prices you estimated, the feeling you tapped, and when each item was noted and decided.
  • What happened at each shield — that it was shown, when, and whether you walked away, waited, opened the shop, let the item go or kept it, and what you said you came for. Your estimated savings, streaks and patterns are made of this.
  • What you add yourself — the spend log, how a purchase felt afterwards, your journal, your own reminders, and the things you chose to do instead, including any app link or web address you gave one.
  • Your rules and settings — danger hours, per-shop rules, your daily goal and limits, blocks, strict-mode state, your pause, your day start, your language and the AI writer you chose. If someone holds your strict-mode key, only a salted one-way hash of their PIN is stored, never the PIN.
  • Usage steps — the highest time step iOS has reported for your protected shops today, for your goal and limits (see below).
  • Your Premium status — the plan and when it renews or ends, so the app knows what to unlock.
  • The latest weekly write-up — only while the AI reflection is on.

If you use iCloud Backup or an encrypted backup to a computer, Apple may include On Ice's data in that backup under your own Apple Account and Apple's privacy terms. We have no access to it.

Your journal

The journal is the one place in On Ice you write for nobody. Entries — and the mood, item or feeling attached to one — are stored on your device and never leave it: not with the AI reflection, not with OpenAI, not with any setting.

  • Lock journal asks for Face ID, Touch ID or your device passcode before the journal opens, and closes it again when you leave the app. iOS performs the check; On Ice never sees your face, fingerprint or passcode. The lock guards the screen; the entries themselves are protected by your device's own encryption, like the rest of On Ice's data.
  • Reflect on my week summarises your recent entries with Apple Intelligence, on your device. There is no server version of it.
  • Export journal makes a plain-text file and hands it to iOS, so you choose where to save it. On Ice sends it nowhere.
  • The journal widget shows only the day's question. It never reads an entry.

Screen Time and your shops

On Ice is built on Apple's Screen Time framework (Family Controls, Managed Settings and Device Activity). You grant it access once, through Apple's own prompt, and can withdraw it at any time in Settings → Screen Time.

When you pick apps, categories or websites in Apple's picker, it returns tokens: values that identify them to iOS but mean nothing to On Ice and nothing outside your device. On Ice stores those tokens so it can ask iOS to shield what you chose; iOS draws their names and icons for you. On Ice never receives a bundle identifier or an app name. Where its own components need to compare tokens, they compare one-way hashes of them, never the tokens themselves.

Websites you add by address are different. A site you type, or pick from On Ice's suggestions, is stored as that address — example.com — because iOS needs it to shield the site. It stays on your device with everything else and is never sent.

Usage is reported in steps. iOS never gives On Ice a total of your screen time. It only tells On Ice when time in your protected shops passes a step registered in advance — five minutes, ten, fifteen… — which is enough for a daily goal and a limit, and nothing more.

On Ice does not observe your browsing, your messages or what you do inside any app. iOS itself draws the shield and enforces it. On Ice learns only that its own shield was shown and what you chose on it.

The AI reflection — the one thing that can leave

The AI reflection is an optional Premium feature. It is off when you install On Ice and stays off until you switch it on in Settings → AI reflection.

With it on, some noted items get one short question before you choose — not every item, only when there is a reason: a feeling you have often regretted, a danger hour, or a larger price. On Sundays it can also write a few sentences about your week.

Two writers, and only one of them leaves the device

  • Apple Intelligence writes on your device. Nothing is sent anywhere. If Apple Intelligence becomes unavailable, On Ice shows you the ordinary choices instead — it never falls back to a server.
  • OpenAI writes on OpenAI's servers, through On Ice's own service. Only what is listed below is sent.

You choose under Reflection writer. If you switch the AI reflection on without choosing, On Ice uses Apple Intelligence where your device and language support it, and OpenAI where they do not.

What is sent, with OpenAI as the writer

For a question:

  • The name of the item you just typed, and anything you write in answer to the question.
  • A price band — a range such as “250 to 500”, never the amount you typed — and your currency.
  • The feeling you tapped, if you tapped one.
  • The hour and minute on your clock. No date, no time zone.
  • How many shopping shields you have met today.
  • Your own regret rate for that one feeling, as a percentage — only once you have rated at least five purchases.
  • Your language, such as “English (UK)”, so the question comes back in it.

For the weekly write-up, counts and totals only: shields met, items you waited on, let go, kept or walked away from, how often you opened a shop, your current and best streak, your busiest hour, the share of items you let go, your estimated savings for the week rounded to a whole number with its currency, the date the week began, and your language. No item names and no individual prices.

What is never sent

  • Screen Time tokens or their hashes, bundle identifiers, or website addresses.
  • Which shops or websites you protect, or how many.
  • The exact price you typed.
  • Anything else on your list — no names, no prices.
  • Your spend log, or any individual purchase price.
  • How you rated any individual purchase.
  • Your journal, rules, limits, blocks, danger hours or strict-mode state.
  • Any name, account, email or advertising identifier — nothing that identifies you as a person.

Where it goes, and how long it is kept

Requests go to On Ice's own service, hosted on Cloudflare, and on to OpenAI, which runs the model. There is no API key inside the app to be extracted, and the app never calls OpenAI directly.

Before answering, the service uses Apple's App Attest to check that the request comes from a genuine copy of On Ice. That check rests on a key created on your device — not on your name or your Apple Account. The service stores no question, answer or item detail. It keeps a count of requests per device per day, under that random key identifier, and deletes it after two days; daily totals of requests and model usage for the whole service, which name no device, for about 90 days; and a record of each one-time App Attest challenge until it expires. Its logs record a request's status, how long it took and how many tokens it used — never its contents.

OpenAI does not use this data to train its models. We switch off OpenAI's stored responses; OpenAI may still keep inputs and outputs in its abuse-monitoring logs, normally for up to 30 days, with exceptions under its own terms. We do not promise that your text is processed in any particular country.

Earlier versions of On Ice send the same kinds of details to Google's Gemini model on Vertex AI instead, through an earlier On Ice service on Google Cloud that also stores no content. Google does not use them to train its models, may keep them for up to 24 hours, and may log them to check for abuse.

Switching the AI reflection off stops every future request at once, deletes the weekly write-up from your device, and removes the Sunday reminder. On Ice goes back to making no network calls of its own. Settings → AI reflection → What gets sent shows the same list inside the app.

Purchases and family plans

Premium is sold through Apple's App Store using StoreKit. The transaction is between you and Apple, under Apple's privacy policy. Apple tells On Ice which plan is active and when it renews or ends — nothing else — and On Ice tells Apple nothing about how you use the app. We never see a card.

With a family plan, Apple's Family Sharing gives each member Premium in their own copy of On Ice. Nothing passes between those copies — no shops, list, journal, savings or settings — and On Ice learns only that Premium was shared, not who shared it.

Notifications, widgets and the Lock Screen

Notifications are optional and are all scheduled on your device. None is sent from a server, and there is no push service. On Ice uses them to hand you from the shield into the app, to say when an item's wait is over or nearly over, for follow-up questions you can switch off in Settings → Follow-up questions, for activity timers and suggestions you choose, and — only with the AI reflection on — for the Sunday write-up. A notification about an item names the item.

Widgets, Live Activities and Control Center controls read a small snapshot on your device: totals, counts, your streak, and the names and times of up to three of the soonest items. They have no Screen Time access and never see which shops you chose. The “On ice” widget and an item's Live Activity show the item's name, so on a Lock Screen anyone holding the device can read it. Money amounts are hidden there while the device is locked. Put them where you are comfortable seeing them.

Third parties

On Ice contains no third-party code: no analytics, no advertising, no attribution, no crash reporter, no SDKs. Only these parties are involved, and only as described here:

  • Apple — operates the App Store and Family Sharing, processes purchases, enforces the Screen Time shield, runs Apple Intelligence on your device, and provides App Attest. If you have opted in to sharing with developers, Apple may share crash logs with us under its own policy.
  • Cloudflare — hosts On Ice's AI service. When the AI reflection uses OpenAI, requests pass through it, and it stores none of their content.
  • OpenAI — runs the model behind OpenAI reflections, only while that writer is in use, and receives only what is listed above.
  • Google Cloud — plays that part for earlier versions of the app only.

We do not sell, rent or share personal data with anyone, and we share nothing with data brokers or advertisers. A link you give a “thing to do instead” opens the app or website you chose, under that app's or site's own policy.

Deleting your data

Everything On Ice holds is on your device, so removing it does not require asking us. You can delete journal entries, spend-log entries, rules, limits, reminders and activities one by one in the app; switching the AI reflection off deletes the weekly write-up; and deleting the app removes everything at once. There is no single button inside the app that erases your whole history, so deleting the app is the way to start fresh. The Your data page walks through each.

On our side there is nothing to delete: the AI service keeps no content, and its per-device count disappears on its own after two days.

Your rights

Wherever you live — including under the GDPR, the UK GDPR and the CCPA/CPRA — you have rights to access, correct, export and erase personal data a company holds about you, to restrict or object to its processing, and to complain to a supervisory authority. On Ice keeps no profile of you on any server, so there is nothing on our side to produce or delete; the data on your device is already in your hands. Using OpenAI reflections is your choice, and switching the AI reflection off withdraws it at once. If you believe we hold something about you, write to topsol.org@gmail.com and we will respond.

Children

On Ice is a self-directed tool for the person using the device. It is not a parental-control product and does not manage another person's device; a family plan shares Premium, never control or data. It is not directed at children under 13 (or the age of digital consent where you live), and we do not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, of any age.
