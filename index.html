How On Ice works

On Ice puts one night between you and the buy button.

You choose the shopping apps and websites that get you. From then on, opening one does not take you straight to the checkout — On Ice's shield comes first: Pause before you buy. Tap Note it to write the thing down, or Never mind to walk away. Nothing is ever bought or cancelled on your behalf.

Anything you note down waits on your list — twenty-four hours, unless you chose another length. When the wait is over, On Ice asks one question: still want it? Most wants don't survive the night, and the ones that do were real. Either answer is a good one.

  • ProtectionOne switch on the home screen, and one for each shop. While it is on, the shops you chose show On Ice's shield instead of opening — the app and the website, in Safari too.
  • A short pauseBefore anything else, a moment to breathe, type a sentence, read a reminder or say what you came for. You choose which, and how long.
  • Note itName the thing, tap a rough price, tap how you feel — bored, stressed, celebrating, deal FOMO, or “I need it”. One screen, fifteen seconds.
  • On iceNoted items wait under a countdown ring. Nothing is bought while an item cools. If you really need the shop now, it opens for a few minutes and the shield comes back on its own.
  • Estimated savingsEvery item you let go counts toward a running total, built entirely from prices you typed yourself — for today, this week and this month.
  • Goals, limits and blocksA daily shopping-time goal, daily limits, timed blocks, Focus blocks, and rules for the hours that go badly. Strict mode holds your setup in place.
  • Something else to doA private journal, and a few things to do instead — a walk, a book, a call — for when the craving needs somewhere to go.
  • Widgets, Live Activities and ShortcutsEstimated savings and what's on ice on the Home Screen and Lock Screen, a countdown while a shop is open, and Siri and Shortcuts actions to start a block.

Requirements

DeviceiPhone or iPad running iOS 17 or newer. A few extras need more: Control Center controls need iOS 18, and writing with Apple Intelligence needs a device and language that support it.
PermissionScreen Time access, granted once during setup through Apple's own prompt. On Ice uses Apple's Screen Time framework — the same system behind iOS's built-in app limits — and Apple, not On Ice, is what actually stands in front of a shop.
AccountNone. There is no sign-up, no email field and no password anywhere in the app.
InternetNot needed. Every shield, pause, list, timer and total works offline. The only network traffic is Apple's when you buy or restore Premium — and the optional AI reflection, if you switch it on with OpenAI as its writer.
LanguagesEnglish, Deutsch, Español, Français, 日本語, 한국어, Português (Brasil) and 简体中文. On Ice follows your device's language, or the one you choose in Settings → Language.
OptionalNotifications, generated on the device, to hand you from the shield into the app and to tell you when an item's wait is over. Motion & Fitness, only to show live steps during a walk. Face ID or Touch ID, only if you lock your journal.

Getting started

Setup is a handful of screens and ends with protection already on.

  1. Try a ten-second pause — the same moment the shield will give you later.
  2. Allow Screen Time access when Apple asks. On Ice cannot work without it, and you can withdraw it any time in Settings → Screen Time.
  3. Pick your shops in Apple's picker, and add the shopping websites you want shielded. On Ice never learns the names of the apps you pick — see Privacy.
  4. Set danger hours if you want them — the late hours when shopping goes badly. Optional, and changeable later.
  5. Done. Open one of your shops and meet the shield.
The first shield can feel abrupt. That is the moment the app exists for — tap Note it and take fifteen seconds.

About the savings number

Every price in On Ice is one you typed yourself, and every money figure the app shows is labelled estimated. That is not a disclaimer bolted on afterwards — it is the whole design.

  • On Ice does not connect to your bank, read your purchase history, check what anything actually costs, or convert between currencies.
  • An item counts toward the total once, when you let it go after its wait.
  • If you never come back to an item, On Ice reminds you, and 48 hours after its wait ends it counts the item as let go. You can decide any time before then.
  • Items you keep don't touch the total — unless you later tell On Ice you bought one for less than you guessed, in which case the difference counts.
  • The spend log is for impulse buys On Ice never saw. It is kept separate from your savings.
The number is a record of what you decided not to buy, at the prices you guessed. It is a good number to be proud of and a bad number to put in a spreadsheet.

Frequently asked questions

Is On Ice free?
Yes, and free is a working product rather than a demo: one shopping app and one website shielded, the twenty-four-hour wait and your list, your estimated savings, the breathing pause, three things to do instead, the journal, a daily shopping-time goal, and timed and Focus blocks.
What does Premium add?
Every shop and website you want to shield; waits of 12, 24, 48 or 72 hours; every kind of pause, pauses that grow with repeated attempts, and your own reminders; danger-hour and per-shop rules; daily limits; strict mode; your shopping patterns and how purchases felt afterwards; a look back at your journal; six things to do instead; widgets; and the optional AI reflection. Premium is a monthly or yearly subscription — yearly starts with a free trial where one is offered — or a single lifetime purchase, and yearly and lifetime also come as family plans. Prices are shown in the app for your country, and billing is handled by Apple.
How do the family plans work?
Premium Family Yearly and Premium Family Lifetime are shared through Apple's Family Sharing with up to five other people in your family group — six in all. Each person gets Premium in their own copy of On Ice, on their own device. Nobody sees anyone else's shops, list, journal or savings.
Does it stop me from buying things?
No. An ordinary shield is a pause, not a wall: tap Note it, say what caught your eye, and I need to shop now opens the shop for a few minutes. I'm done — block it again closes it again early. The only things with no way through are the ones you set up yourself — a hard-block rule, a daily limit that has been reached, a timed block or a Focus block — and each of them ends on its own.
What happens if my subscription lapses?
Nothing you built is taken away. Your list, history and estimated savings stay, the shops you chose stay shielded, and your rules, limits and a running strict mode keep holding. Your wait goes back to 24 hours, the AI reflection switches off, and widgets and the pattern screens wait for Premium to return. You just can't add more Premium features.
Can I restore a purchase on a new device?
Yes. Open Settings and tap Restore purchases. Premium belongs to your Apple Account, not to the device. Your list, journal and history do not travel with it — they live on the device that made them, and move to a new one only with a backup of the whole device.
Does it work on iPad?
Yes — iPhone and iPad, iOS 17 or newer. Each device keeps its own shops, list and history; nothing syncs between them.
Why can't On Ice tell me which apps I protected?
Because it genuinely does not know. Apple's Screen Time hands On Ice opaque tokens that are meaningless outside your device — no app names, no identifiers. On Ice can ask iOS to shield them, and iOS draws their names and icons for you; On Ice cannot read them. Websites you add by typing an address are the exception: On Ice keeps that address on your device so iOS can shield it.
Does it shield websites, or only apps?
Both. Add a shop's app and its website separately, and each is shielded — the website in Safari.
Does On Ice see what I buy?
No. It has no access to your bank, your orders, your browsing or your card. It records only its own events — that a shield was shown, and what you chose — and keeps those on your device.
Does my journal leave my device?
Never. Not to us, not to OpenAI, not with any setting. You can lock it with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode, and its optional weekly summary is written by Apple Intelligence on your device.
Where do my walking steps go?
Nowhere. During a walk On Ice shows your steps live, and forgets them when the walk ends. It does not read from or write to Apple Health.
What is the AI reflection, and is it on?
It is one short question before the choices, now and then, and a few sentences about your week on Sundays. It is off until you turn it on. You choose who writes it: Apple Intelligence, on your device, or OpenAI — the only thing in the app that sends anything off the device. The Privacy Policy lists exactly what.

Troubleshooting

A shop opens without a shield

  • Check that protection is on at the top of the home screen, and that the shop's own switch is on.
  • Check that Screen Time access is still granted: Settings → Screen Time → Apps with Screen Time Access. If it was withdrawn, On Ice shows a screen with a way to ask again.
  • A shopping window you opened may still be running. It closes on its own — or tap I'm done — block it again — and the shield comes back.
  • If the shop is covered only by a rule, the shield appears only during that rule's hours.
  • Still nothing? Settings → Protection tools → Repair protection applies every shield and schedule again.

Tapping “Note it” doesn't open On Ice

  • On some versions of iOS the shield hands you over with a notification. If notifications are off, iOS cannot deliver it — turn them on in Settings → Notifications → On Ice.
  • Opening On Ice yourself picks up the item waiting to be noted.

A website isn't shielded

Websites are shielded by address. Make sure the address you added matches the one you actually open, and remember that a shop's app and its website are two separate things to add.

My daily goal or limit shows fewer minutes than I expected

iOS does not tell apps how long you spent in another app. It only tells On Ice when time in your protected shops passes a step — every five minutes at first — so On Ice shows “at least” that many minutes. The real figure can be a little higher.

I can't turn protection off or remove a shop

Check whether strict mode, a timed block or a Focus block is running — the home screen says so. While strict mode runs, your setup is held in place by design, and it ends on its own at the time you chose. If you set up Someone holds the key, the person with the PIN can end it early.

The AI question never appears

It needs Premium, and it is off until you switch it on in Settings → AI reflection. Even then it asks only when there is a reason — a feeling you have often regretted, a danger hour, or a larger price. With OpenAI as the writer it needs an internet connection. If anything goes wrong it quietly shows you the ordinary choices instead.

Widgets are behind

iOS refreshes widgets on its own schedule. Opening On Ice brings them up to date. The savings and “On ice” widgets come with Premium.

Premium isn't recognised

Tap Restore purchases in Settings, and make sure the device is signed into the same Apple Account that made the purchase — or, for a family plan, that you are in the purchaser's Family Sharing group. Refunds and billing questions are handled by Apple through Apple Support.

The app is in the wrong language

Choose one in Settings → Language. Apple's own app picker follows the next time you open On Ice.
