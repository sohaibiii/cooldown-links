How to stop emotional spending: boredom, stress and the rest

Emotional spending is buying something to change how you feel, not because you need the thing. Everybody does it sometimes, and sometimes it is fine. It becomes a problem when one feeling has learned that the answer is always a shop.

By the On Ice team · Updated 28 November 2026 · 5 min read

The five feelings that shop

You don't need a theory for this. You need to know which of these is yours, because the fix is different for each.

  • Bored. Browsing is something to do with your hands. The parcel is a small event in a flat week. This one is often the easiest to change.
  • Stressed or worn out. After a hard day, choosing something nice feels like getting a bit of control back.
  • Low. This is what people mean by retail therapy. Buying something does lift your mood for a while. It just doesn't last as long as the delivery takes.
  • Celebrating. You earned it. Often you did, and this one is frequently fine.
  • Afraid of missing out. The timer, the last three in stock, the thing everyone else has.

Find yours in two weeks

Guessing doesn't work well here, because the feeling is hard to see from inside it. Measure it.

For two weeks, each time you buy something you hadn't planned, or nearly do, write down three things: what it was, the time, and one word for how you felt just before. One word, chosen quickly. Don't judge it and don't try to change anything yet.

At the end, count the words. You will probably find that one feeling accounts for most of the list, often at the same time of day. That is the one to work on. The others you can leave alone.

Give the feeling something else

A feeling that shops is doing a job for you. Telling it to stop doesn't work. Giving it a different way to do the job does.

If you feelWhat the shop was giving youTry first
BoredSomething to doTen minutes of anything with your hands or feet: a walk, the washing up, a call
StressedA sense of controlOne small thing you can finish, or two minutes of slow breathing
LowA liftA person. Message someone before you open the shop
CelebratingA rewardChoose the reward on purpose, tomorrow, and enjoy it
Missing outRelief from the timerAsk whether you would buy it at this price with no timer

Keep the replacement small and close to hand. "Go to the gym" loses to a checkout every time. "Walk to the end of the road" sometimes wins.

Wait for the feeling to pass, not the want

Feelings move faster than wants. Boredom lasts an hour. A bad day ends. If you put a night between the feeling and the purchase, you get to decide in a different mood from the one that picked the thing.

So write it down, with the price, and look again tomorrow. That is the 24 hour rule, and emotional spending is what it is best at. If you still want it when the feeling has gone, it was never the feeling. Buy it.

Sometimes the answer is yes

A treat after a hard week is not a failure of anything. The aim here is not to stop buying things that make you happy. It is to notice when one feeling has started spending for you without asking, and to give yourself the choice back. Nothing on this page works by making you feel bad, and guilt after a purchase has never yet prevented the next one.

When to talk to someone

If shopping is how you cope with feelings that don't lift, or it is causing debt or arguments you can't get on top of, please talk to someone qualified, such as your doctor or a counsellor. That is a different thing from a habit, and it deserves better help than an article.

How On Ice helps with this

We make On Ice, an iPhone app, so here is our part in it. The two week exercise above is the thing the app does on its own. When you open a shop you've chosen, a shield appears first. Tap Note it and the note asks for a name, a rough price and how you are feeling. That takes about fifteen seconds, and it happens at the one moment the feeling is easy to name.

From there you can also go and do something else: a walk, a few pages, two minutes of breathing. Pick the small things you would enjoy, and one tap starts them.

The thing you noted waits 24 hours, and the app asks about it once, when the mood has had time to change. With Premium you can also see your shopping patterns over time, including how purchases felt afterwards.

It never tells you off. If you still want the thing tomorrow, the shop opens.
