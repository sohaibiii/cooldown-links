How to stop impulse buying online: 8 things that work

Impulse buying is rarely about the thing. It is about speed. You see it, you want it, and the shop has made sure nothing stands between the two. Everything that works does the same job: it puts something in between.

By the On Ice team · Updated 28 November 2026 · 6 min read

None of these ask for more self control. Self control is what you run out of at 11 p.m., which is when it is needed. They are ordered from the quickest to set up to the most complete, and you don't need all eight. Two or three that suit you will do more than the full list done badly.

1. Take your card out of the shop

A saved card turns buying into one tap. Remove it from the shops you overuse, in each shop's account or payment settings. Then remove it from Safari: open Settings, find Safari (on newer versions of iOS it sits under Apps), tap AutoFill, then Saved Credit Cards.

Typing sixteen digits is a small job. It is also the only pause most checkouts have left, and it is often enough.

2. Switch off one tap ordering

Many shops have an express checkout that skips the basket and the review page. Where the shop lets you turn it off, do. An order that has to go through the basket gives you one more screen on which to change your mind.

3. Silence the invitations

Most impulse buys start with the shop contacting you, not the other way round. Open Settings, tap Notifications, pick each shopping app and switch off Allow Notifications. Unsubscribe from marketing emails as they arrive, one at a time. Within a week your phone asks you to buy things far less often.

4. Wait one night

If you didn't plan to buy it, wait a day. Wants are strongest in the first minutes and most are gone by morning, so this one rule filters out a great deal. It has well known weak spots, and we cover them, with a way to do it by hand, in the 24 hour rule for shopping.

5. Shop from a list you wrote earlier

When you do need to buy things, write down what before you open the shop. Inside the shop, the list is the only question: is this on it? Everything else you see there is the shop's list, not yours.

6. Learn your hour and your feeling

For two weeks, each time you buy something unplanned, note the time and one word for how you felt. A pattern shows up quickly, and it is usually narrow: the same hour, the same mood, often the same shop. A narrow problem needs a narrow fix. If yours is the evening, see how to stop late night online shopping. If it is a mood, see how to stop emotional spending.

7. Give the want somewhere to go

A want that has nowhere to go sits in your head until you buy the thing to make it stop. Give it a place: one note on your phone with the item and a rough price. Not the shop's own wish list or basket, because the shop will email you about those within the hour. Writing it down is very often all the want needed.

8. Put a pause on the door

The first seven change what happens inside the shop or inside your head. The last one changes what happens when you open the shop at all.

Your iPhone can do a version of this with Screen Time: a daily limit on an app or a full block on a website. The steps are in how to block Amazon on iPhone and they are the same for any shop. The weakness is the Ignore Limit For Today button, which is one tap with no pause before it.

What usually doesn't work

  • Deciding to be stronger. It works on the days you didn't need it.
  • Deleting the app for the fourth time. If it keeps coming back, the app isn't the problem. We compare the options in On Ice vs deleting your shopping apps.
  • Feeling bad afterwards. Guilt arrives after the order and changes nothing about the next one. A slower checkout does more than a sterner conscience.

If it feels bigger than a habit

Everything here is for the ordinary kind of buying too much. If shopping is causing debt you can't keep up with, or real distress, please talk to someone qualified as well, such as your doctor or a free debt advice service in your country. A list of tips is not a substitute for that.
